News and Wikipedia: experiment comparison

This is a report on three domain experiments, not the final assignment report.

Each experiment trains orders 1, 2 and 3 at 100K, 1M and 10M word budgets. The 500 evaluation records, seed 42, normalization, perplexity denominator, BOS/EOS and first-index tie rule are fixed. All 27 new models were evaluated. The original nine models are retained as a reference.

Interpretation: candidate 0 is always correct, so the first-candidate baseline is 100%. Seeded random accuracy is 15.6%. Random top-tie accuracy below is an analytical expectation from saved equal top scores; official predictions have not been changed.

Enlarged caches retain up to 750,000 normalized unique sentences per domain, using the same seeded hash-priority rule. Smaller subsets remain nested whole-sentence prefixes. News+Wikipedia uses round-robin sentence mixing and cross-domain deduplication; actual word shares are recorded below.

Observed findings

News-only has the highest accuracy in 9/9 matched size/order comparisons. Its best accuracy is 92.8%, 2.6 percentage points above the original five-domain best. This is consistent with news training text matching the evaluation titles more closely.

At 10M words, the news bigram scores 92.8% and the news trigram scores 92.8%. Their WER values are 0.87% and 0.89%, and binary sizes are 85.33 and 239.38 MiB, respectively. The bigram offers a favorable storage/accuracy trade-off in this run.

At 10M words, the news+wiki corpus has reference OOV 1.63%, versus 1.67% for news alone, while its trigram accuracy is 91.6%. Improved vocabulary coverage alone therefore does not guarantee better selection.

Comparisons are descriptive: the best models are selected on this same 500-record set. One seed and one timing pass were used. Corpus size and order are controlled, but source vocabulary and phrase distributions differ. Exact-sentence leakage checks do not exclude all partial or semantic overlap. Model bytes are disk size, not measured RAM. Single-run latency includes normalization/scoring/ranking and excludes loading, metrics and output I/O.
