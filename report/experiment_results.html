Ukrainian ASR candidate rescoring

Classical n-gram language models | 24 November 2026
Run: 20261124_main

Result. The best language model was a trigram trained on 9,999,984 words: 90.2% exact-match accuracy (451/500), WER 1.35%, CER 0.69%, and MRR 0.9498. Increasing corpus size improved all three n-gram orders.

Critical interpretation. The reference occurs as candidate 0 in every record. Consequently, selecting the first candidate achieves 100% accuracy and zero WER/CER. The experiment demonstrates language-model ranking behavior on synthetic ASR-like candidates; it does not establish an improvement over the provided candidate order.

1. Corpus preparation and scoring

Five official sentence-split bz2 exports (5.23 GB compressed) were scanned once: 156,053,480 nonempty sentence occurrences. Each domain retained up to 200,000 unique normalized sentences using seeded hash-priority sampling. The retained domain samples contain 19,945,723 words before cross-domain deduplication. Interleaving and global deduplication produced 998,334 sentences.

Mixture weights are approximately equal by sentence count, not word count. Court sentences are longer and supply about 40% of the 10M words. Smaller subsets are strict whole-sentence prefixes; the final sentence is never cut or skipped to force an exact budget. All models of a given size use exactly the same text.

Normalization. Apply Unicode NFC, lowercase, canonical apostrophes, Unicode letter/number tokenization with internal apostrophes, and whitespace collapse. Other punctuation becomes separators. No spelling correction, transliteration or stemming is applied. Corpus-only cleaning rejects URLs/obvious markup, non-Cyrillic lines, and sentences outside 2–150 words. Segmentation uses the upstream sentenced export.

Leakage audit. Normalized evaluation references and hypotheses are exclusions only. Exact training overlaps are removed before sampling; the final subsets passed a zero-overlap audit. Related source documents, partial overlaps and paraphrases are not excluded by this exact-sentence check.

Scoring. L = log10 P(sentence + EOS | BOS); H = −L/N; perplexity = 10H. N counts normalized words and excludes boundary tokens, following the assignment. KenLM’s built-in perplexity instead includes EOS in N and is not used [4]. Candidates are ranked by H to avoid exponent overflow; exact ties use original order.

Unigrams. KenLM estimates order 1, but its generic ARPA loader requires a higher-order container. A container with zero observed bigrams and zero backoffs preserves the original unigram probabilities. KenLM context is reset per word/EOS; native smoke tests verify the resulting sums against the source unigram ARPA. Bigram/trigram models use modified Kneser–Ney estimation [3], without pruning or fallback discounts. The scoring convention is fixed for the entire grid.

From 100K to 10M words, accuracy increases by 3.8 percentage points for unigrams, 11.4 points for bigrams, and 11.6 points for trigrams. At 10M, adding context from unigram to trigram yields 12.8 points, while trigram over bigram adds only 0.8 points (four records). More data has a larger effect than adding the third order after bigrams are available.

The best model leaves 49 exact-match errors, but its reference is still ranked second in 46 of them. Its low WER/CER reflects mostly small local changes in otherwise correct titles. Oracle accuracy is 100% for every configuration; failures are ranking errors, not missing-reference cases. The test set is reused to compare all nine models, so “best” is a descriptive selection rather than an independent generalization estimate.

Reference OOV drops from 28.08% (100K) to 8.97% (1M) to 2.32% (10M). Candidate OOV falls from 35.33% to 10.87%. At a fixed corpus size the vocabulary is shared across orders, so higher-order gains cannot be attributed to a larger vocabulary.

All nine models required 33.56 seconds of recorded training/build time. The successful runner completed in approximately 60.5 seconds (including validation, hashes, loading, metrics and exports). The 10M trigram occupies 224.18 MiB, versus 86.30 MiB for the bigram: about 2.60× the storage for four extra correct records. The bigram is therefore an attractive resource/accuracy compromise on this evaluation.

These are single-run timings with warmed scoring after one warmup, on a Linux container with a 2G KenLM sorting-memory setting. Scoring includes normalization and ranking; loading, metrics, OOV scans and export are excluded. Per-token Python calls add unigram overhead. No repeated latency benchmark, peak-RSS measurement, GPU comparison, or speed-significance claim is made. An initial native crash was resolved by using Linux-local scratch files and copying completed models to Windows; the failed attempt is excluded from the successful timing.

4. Qualitative successes and failures

A. Context resolves a case-ending error (ua_title_0005)

Reference: Волонтери через брак коштів припиняють вивезення тіл загиблих із зони АТО

Unigram selected: Волонтери через брак коштів припиняють вивезення тіл загиблих із зоні АТО
Trigram: selects the reference; H(ref)=2.9144, H(unigram choice)=3.3107.

The unigram prefers «із зоні АТО»; the trigram favors «із зони АТО». The result is consistent with local context helping select the appropriate case ending rather than relying only on word frequency.

B. Context distinguishes a real-word substitution (ua_title_0246)

Reference: Польща депортує росіянина, який запустив дрон над офісом президента

Unigram selected: Польща депортує росіянина, який запустив трон над офісом президента
Trigram: selects the reference; H(ref)=4.0838, H(unigram choice)=4.2098.

Both «дрон» and «трон» are real words. The trigram selects the intended sentence containing «запустив дрон», while the unigram selects «трон». A fluent individual word can still be a recognition error in context.

C. Repetition can lower average perplexity (ua_title_0024)

Reference: Вчені відтворили звучання відомої середньовічної мелодії

Trigram selected: Вчені відтворили звучання відомої відомої середньовічної мелодії
Perplexity: reference 258,382.2; selected 239,645.0.

The duplicated «відомої» is preferred even though all words are in vocabulary. Optimizing average word cost can reward an extra relatively cheap word. This is a ranking-objective failure.

D. Apostrophe fragmentation changes tokenization (ua_title_0317)

Reference: Джиро д'Італія. Подвійний успіх Берналя

Trigram selected: Джиро д ' Італія. Подвійний успіх Берналя
Perplexity: reference 224,857.5; selected 43,826.7.

The correct internal apostrophe stays within a token, while «д ' Італія» becomes separate tokens. Their different N values and contexts change perplexity. The evaluation deliberately does not repair this recognition error during normalization.

E. Correct vocabulary coverage does not guarantee a correct name (ua_title_0049)

Reference: Атлетіко обіграв Бетіс на виїзді

Trigram selected: Атлетіко обіграв Бетис на виїзді
Perplexity: reference 63,083.3; selected 35,543.8.

Both «Бетіс» and the wrong «Бетис» are in vocabulary, but the model prefers the wrong spelling. More coverage and local fluency do not provide a factual guarantee about a named entity.

Across all 49 best-model errors, 30 are pure adjacent repetitions and 2 are pure apostrophe fragmentation; the remaining 17 are other or combined errors. These categories are deterministic token-pattern diagnostics, not a complete linguistic annotation.

5. Position bias, exact ties and uncertainty

Candidate order is informative in this dataset. Candidate 0 is always the reference. This explains the perfect first-candidate baseline and also benefits first-index tie breaking whenever a reference ties a different normalized sentence. The official results above preserve that configured convention throughout.

Analytical sensitivity diagnostic, not a second scored experiment. For each saved exact top-score tie, the expected correctness under uniform tie selection is the fraction of tied candidates matching the reference. No scores or official predictions are changed. The best model has 28 top ties involving different normalized sentences. Its accuracy can range from 84.8% to 90.2% under different exact-tie choices; uniform top-tie selection gives expected 87.58%.

The small-corpus scores are especially sensitive: the 100K unigram falls from 73.6% recorded accuracy to about 46.18% expected under uniform top-tie selection. Mapping unseen variants to the same unknown token can produce equal scores. Duplicate normalized candidates by themselves are not an accuracy error; the audit counts ties between distinct normalized texts separately.

Uncertainty. A descriptive 95% Wilson interval for the best model's 451/500 correctness is 87.28%–92.51%. This interval conditions on this evaluation convention and does not adjust for selecting the best of nine models. Against the 10M bigram, the trigram wins on 5 records and loses on 1; the two-sided exact McNemar p-value is 0.2188. Four extra correct items are insufficient to establish a reliable superiority claim from this exploratory comparison.

Reference OOV tokens are 1.96% among correct selections and 5.85% among errors. This association supports examining vocabulary coverage in failure analysis, but does not establish causality: length, rare entities and domain also differ.

6. Conclusions, limitations and reproducibility

Answer to the assignment. Higher-order context and additional text improve candidate ranking in this grid. Trigrams lead at every corpus size, and bigram/trigram models benefit much more from a 100-fold corpus increase than unigrams. The 10M trigram gives the highest recorded LM accuracy, while the 10M bigram offers nearly the same accuracy with substantially less storage. Both outperform the seeded random baseline and both underperform the perfect positional baseline.

Where the method works. Learned local co-occurrences help distinguish plausible case endings and real-word substitutions. Where it fails. Fluency scores do not encode the intended utterance, acoustic evidence, or factual name identity. Average perplexity can favor repetitions and fragmented tokens. OOV collapse and the deterministic gold-first tie rule further complicate interpretation.

Limitations and next experiments

The 500 examples are ASR-like title hypotheses rather than an independently decoded speech test set. References are always available and always first. All nine settings were compared on the same evaluation set, with one preparation seed and one timing run. Exact leakage filtering does not remove partial or semantic overlap. Court text supplies about 40% of words; the fixed mixture is not a domain-ablation study. Model size is not peak memory use, and single-run sub-millisecond times should not be treated as production latency estimates.

A stronger follow-up would use an independent held-out set with randomized candidate positions, an explicitly fixed order-neutral tie policy, repeated seeds/timings, and a genuine ASR top-1 baseline. Repetition or word-insertion penalties and acoustic/LM combination should be tuned on separate development data. The optional modern LLM comparison was not run; no neural-model or memory-use results are claimed.
